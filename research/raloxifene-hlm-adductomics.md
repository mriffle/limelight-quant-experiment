# Research: Prior art on proteome-wide raloxifene HLM adductomics and CYP2C adduction

## Summary

One published study — the only one located by the searches performed — performs proteome-wide (unbiased LC-MS/MS-based) mapping of raloxifene covalent protein adducts in human liver microsomes: Zelter et al., *J. Proteome Res.* 2024 (PMID 39442081), which used a DDA+DIA workflow to find raloxifene adducts on 78 HLM proteins (127 peptides), including CYP3A and CYP2C family enzymes and several non-CYP ER/cytosolic proteins [pmid:39442081]. That same study explicitly reports that the tryptic peptide **SPC*MQDR**, shared by CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C18 and CYP2C19, was found raloxifene-adducted in HLM and in every CYP2C recombinant supersome tested [pmid:39442081]. Independent UniProt sequence retrieval confirms that all four CYP2C paralogs (P10632/CYP2C8, P11712/CYP2C9, P33260/CYP2C18, P33261/CYP2C19) contain the exact substring `SPCMQDR` at identical residues 336-342 (Cys at position 338), so the peptide is genuinely non-unique across the CYP2C subfamily — but note the accession-to-isoform mapping in the task prompt for P33260/P33261 is reversed relative to current UniProt records (see Detailed findings). Separately, raloxifene is reported as a time-dependent inactivator of CYP2C8 (not CYP2C9, CYP2C18, or CYP2C19) at a different cysteine, Cys225, per VandenBrink et al. 2012 [pmid:22859722] — a site distinct from the Cys338 in the SPCMQDR peptide, and adduction of the CYP2C9 active-site Cys216 was shown not to cause time-dependent inhibition [pmid:39442081]. Background chemoproteomic/adductomics literature (a probe-based rat-liver-microsome study and a review) supports that non-CYP ER proteins such as MGST1 and protein disulfide isomerases are recurring reactive-metabolite targets [pmid:16167842; pmid:26510387; pmid:31018479].

## Detailed findings

### 1. Is there a proteome-wide/adductomics study of raloxifene adducts in HLM or hepatocytes?

**Yes — one.** Zelter A, Riffle M, Shteynberg DD, Zhong G, Riddle EB, Hoopmann MR, Jaschob D, Moritz RL, Davis TN, MacCoss MJ, Isoherranen N. "Detection and Quantification of Drug-Protein Adducts in Human Liver." *J Proteome Res.* 2024;23(11):5143-5152. doi:10.1021/acs.jproteome.4c00663, PMID 39442081, PMCID PMC11537226.

- **Method**: a hybrid data-dependent acquisition (DDA) + data-independent acquisition (DIA) LC-MS/MS workflow. Peptide identification used a **closed/targeted mass search** for the known raloxifene adduct mass (471.15 Da for raloxifene-d0, 475.18 Da for raloxifene-d4) with the Comet search engine plus PeptideProphet (Variable Modification Count model) and PTMProphet (TPP tools), not an open/unrestricted modification search — i.e., it is targeted to a pre-specified adduct mass, but applied proteome-wide (all peptides in the HLM digest), which is why it counts as "proteome-wide" rather than "single-protein targeted." This is distinct from a true open-modification search; the predecessor open-search tool from the same group (Magnum, in Riffle et al. 2022) was validated on three drug/protein combinations, with the label-free workflow separately applied to discover novel adducts in a reconstituted recombinant CYP3A4 + NADPH-P450 reductase incubation, with novel adducts reported on both proteins (12 residues in CYP3A4, 11 in P450 reductase) — a low-complexity system, not a full HLM proteome [pmid:35184559; pmid:39442081].
- **Scale**: HLMs from 3 individual donors (run and analyzed separately, not pooled) were incubated with raloxifene; of ~2 million peptide-spectrum matches (1% FDR), raloxifene adducts were found in **78 proteins (127 unique adducted peptides)**, described by the authors as "a significant success compared to the 5-6 unique adducted peptides identified in prior acetaminophen studies and the single raloxifene-CYP adduct previously identified in a native membrane environment" [pmid:39442081].
- Adducted protein classes beyond CYPs included "several ER metabolic enzymes (e.g., retinol dehydrogenase, alcohol dehydrogenase, microsomal glutathione S-transferase and carboxylesterase), ribosomal components, and various binding proteins" [pmid:39442081].
- Recombinant confirmation: supersomes (insect-cell microsomes) individually expressing CYP3A4, CYP3A5, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, or CYP2C19 (with P450 reductase and cytochrome b5) were each incubated with raloxifene-d0/d4; adducts were detected in **every** supersome tested, including CYP3A5, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19, none of which show time-dependent inhibition by raloxifene — the authors conclude that "multiple factors" beyond simple adduction (e.g., adduct location, not just adduct presence) govern whether an adduct causes inactivation, i.e., "these data suggest adducts can be benign" [pmid:39442081].

No other proteome-wide/open-search/radiolabel-based *protein-identification* study of raloxifene in HLM or hepatocytes was located. Searches run (WebSearch, PubMed E-utilities, Europe PMC) included: "raloxifene reactive metabolite protein adducts proteome-wide human liver microsomes mass spectrometry"; "raloxifene covalent adduct CYP3A4 Cys239 human liver microsomes mass spectrometry"; "raloxifene radiolabeled covalent binding hepatocytes protein targets identification"; "raloxifene adductomics OR open search proteome hepatocytes covalent binding 2023 2024 2025"; and citation-chasing of the reference list of Zelter et al. 2024 (Europe PMC full-text XML, PMC11537226). Two adjacent but non-proteome-wide precedents were found and are useful contrast/context, not counterexamples to the "one proteome-wide study" conclusion:
  - **Liu et al. 2005** [pmid:16167842] used a synthetic biotin-tagged raloxifene-derived probe ("COATag") in **rat** liver microsomes; affinity-captured, NADPH-dependent covalently modified proteins were identified as GRP78/BiP, three protein disulfide isomerases, and MGST1. This is a multi-protein but **probe/affinity-capture-limited** (not unbiased whole-proteome) method, in rat rather than human microsomes, and no CYP was among the proteins it identified.
  - **Obach et al. 2008** [pmid:18690722] quantified bulk covalent-binding of raloxifene (among 18 drugs) to human liver microsomal protein (conventionally a radiolabel-based readout in this literature, though the use of radiolabeled compound specifically was not directly confirmed in retrievable text for this paywalled paper), but this is a total covalent-binding measurement, not a protein- or site-level identification method.
  - Earlier raloxifene-CYP3A4 work (Chen et al. 2002, PMID 12119000; Baer et al. 2007, PMID 17497897; Yukinaga et al. 2007, PMID 17867646) identified the single Cys239 adduct in purified/reconstituted or recombinant CYP3A4 — single-protein targeted MS, explicitly excluded from this report's scope per instructions.

**Conclusion for Q1**: a proteome-wide adductomics study of raloxifene in HLM exists and is directly on point (Zelter et al. 2024); no second, independent proteome-wide study, and no hepatocyte-based (as opposed to microsome-based) proteome-wide study, was located by the searches above.

### 2. CYP2C adduction and time-dependent inhibition; the SPCMQDR peptide

**Adduction of CYP2C enzymes**: Yes, directly reported. In Zelter et al. 2024, 15 raloxifene-adducted peptides were found across 9 CYP enzymes in HLM (10 unique to one CYP, 5 shared across CYPs owing to ≥40% subfamily sequence identity) [pmid:39442081]. The paper explicitly names CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C18 and CYP2C19 in connection with the shared peptide SPC*MQDR, and separately reports a CYP2C9-specific active-site adduct at Cys216 (peptide `ILSSPWIQIC*NNFSPIIDYFPGTHNK`) [pmid:39442081]. (Note: the paper's running text names 6 human CYP gene symbols explicitly — CYP3A4, CYP3A5, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C18, CYP2C19; the full enumeration of all "9 CYP enzymes" is given in Figure 1c / Supplementary Table S1, which could not be extracted from the article's plain-text/XML body in this research pass — flagged as not independently verified beyond the 6 named isoforms.)

Recombinant-supersome confirmation of CYP2C adduction covered **CYP2C8, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19** (CYP2C18 was not one of the 5 supersome systems tested — CYP3A4, CYP3A5, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 — so CYP2C18's contribution to the shared HLM peptide rests on peptide-sequence identity/inference, not a CYP2C18-specific recombinant experiment) [pmid:39442081].

**Time-dependent inhibition (TDI) of CYP2C enzymes**: Yes, for **CYP2C8 only**, per VandenBrink et al. 2012 (*Mol Pharmacol*, PMID 22859722), cited by Zelter et al. 2024 as the source of this claim. VandenBrink et al. screened raloxifene against CYP1A2, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, CYP3A5 (in addition to the already-known CYP3A4) and found that every P450 except CYP2E1 formed raloxifene-glutathione adducts (bioactivation-competent), but **time-dependent (mechanism-based) inactivation occurred only in CYP2C8 and CYP3A4** (KI = 0.26 µM, kinact = 0.10 min⁻¹ for CYP2C8; KI = 0.81 µM, kinact = 0.20 min⁻¹ for CYP3A4) [pmid:22859722]. CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 bioactivate raloxifene (form GSH adducts) but are **not** time-dependently inhibited [pmid:22859722; pmid:39442081]. CYP2C18 does not appear to have been tested for TDI in either paper (it was not part of the VandenBrink 2012 panel nor the Zelter 2024 supersome panel); no TDI report for CYP2C18 was located.

**Important distinction — two different cysteines are involved, and adduction ≠ inactivation**: VandenBrink et al. 2012 localized the CYP2C8 inactivation-associated adduct to **Cys225** (identified by proteolytic digestion of raloxifene-treated CYP2C8 Supersomes) [pmid:22859722] — a different residue from the Cys338 found in the shared SPCMQDR peptide reported by Zelter et al. 2024. VandenBrink et al. also mapped substrate access channels and active-site cysteines across the CYP panel and found that only CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 have an accessible cysteine near the active-site cavity, which the authors propose explains why only these two isoforms are inactivated despite broader bioactivation [pmid:22859722]. Separately, Zelter et al. 2024 directly demonstrate that adduction of an active-site cysteine does not necessarily cause inactivation: CYP2C9's active-site Cys216 was adducted by raloxifene along with 5 other adducted residues, yet none of these adducts — including Cys216 — produced time-dependent inhibition of CYP2C9 [pmid:39442081]. Taken together, an adducted Cys338 (SPCMQDR) in CYP2C8/2C9/2C18/2C19 has, at present, no reported functional/TDI consequence attached to it specifically — the TDI-linked cysteine in CYP2C8 (Cys225) and the adductomics-detected shared peptide cysteine (Cys338) are distinct sites in distinct experiments.

### 3. Background: state of the art for covalent-adductomics of drug reactive metabolites in microsomes, and non-CYP hepatic targets

Untargeted/semi-targeted MS-based "adductomics" of reactive-metabolite protein adducts in liver microsomes is a small and still-developing field, reviewed by Nunes et al. (2019, *High Throughput*, PMID 31018479), who describe both targeted (adducts arising from exposure to a specific chemical agent) and untargeted ('adductomics'; the totality of conjugates present at a given nucleophilic residue) MS strategies for protein covalent-adduct identification, and note that while MS offers high sensitivity, low-abundance adducts remain challenging to detect, along with bias toward abundant proteins, in complex matrices [pmid:31018479]. Open-modification-search tooling built specifically for this problem (Magnum/Limelight) was introduced by the same UW/ISB group that later produced the raloxifene HLM study; the tools themselves were validated on three drug/protein combinations, with the label-free workflow separately applied to discover novel adducts in a reconstituted recombinant CYP3A4 + NADPH-P450 reductase incubation, with novel adducts reported on both proteins (12 residues in CYP3A4, 11 in P450 reductase) — a low-complexity system, rather than a full microsomal proteome [pmid:35184559]. Affinity/probe-based chemoproteomic strategies are an older, complementary approach: a biotin-tagged raloxifene-derived probe ("COATag") applied to rat liver microsomes identified GRP78/BiP, three protein disulfide isomerases, and microsomal glutathione S-transferase 1 (MGST1) as covalently modified, NADPH-dependent targets — i.e., non-CYP ER/chaperone proteins, not CYPs, in that probe-limited experiment [pmid:16167842]. Consistent with this, microsomal glutathione S-transferases recur as reactive-metabolite targets across different drugs and species: the rat MGST1 active-site cysteine (C50) was independently identified as covalently adducted by acetaminophen in a 2D-LC-MS/MS rat-liver-microsome adductomics study [pmid:26510387], and the human MGST1 homologous peptide/cysteine was independently found adducted by raloxifene in the Zelter et al. 2024 HLM study, at the residue equivalent to the acetaminophen-adducted rat site [pmid:39442081]. Together these sources support that non-CYP hepatic ER proteins — MGST1 and protein disulfide isomerases in particular — are recurrently reported reactive-metabolite targets in microsomal covalent-adductomics/chemoproteomics studies, not an artifact unique to raloxifene.
